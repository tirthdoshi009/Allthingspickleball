# Pickleball-specific books to investigate

The current book notes mostly draw from adjacent sports books and translate them to
pickleball. There are also books written directly for pickleball players. Based on a
small survey of recurring titles in pickleball reading lists, these are the most useful
ones to investigate first.

## Shortlist

| Book | Author | Why it belongs on the list |
|---|---|---|
| *The Art of Pickleball: Techniques and Strategies for Everyone* | Gale H. Leach | One of the older general guides devoted fully to pickleball, with technique, strategy, equipment, and practice material. |
| *At the Line Pickleball: The Winning Doubles Pickleball Strategy* | Joe Baker | A doubles-strategy book, which makes it especially relevant because doubles positioning, partner coverage, and shot selection are where most pickleball points are decided. |
| *Smart Pickleball: The Pickleball Guru's Guide* | Prem Carnot | A strategy-first guide focused on playing higher-percentage pickleball rather than just collecting more strokes. |
| *Pickleball Fundamentals* | Mary Littlewood with USA Pickleball Association | A fundamentals manual worth checking as a baseline reference for rules, strokes, tactics, and drills. |
| *How to Play Pickleball: The Complete Guide from A to Z* | Joe Baker and Richard "Coach Mo" Movsessian | A broad instruction book from two well-known coaches, useful if the goal is a beginner-to-intermediate roadmap. |
| *Pickleball Mindset: The Blueprint for Peak Performance* | Dayne Gingrich and Jill Martin | A pickleball-specific mental-game book, making it a natural follow-up to the existing notes on *The Inner Game of Tennis* and *Mind Gym*. |

## What to read first

Start with one general manual, one doubles-strategy book, and one mental-game book:

1. *The Art of Pickleball* for the broad baseline.
2. *At the Line Pickleball* or *Smart Pickleball* for doubles decision-making.
3. *Pickleball Mindset* for pressure, focus, confidence, and partner dynamics in a
   pickleball context.

That combination should answer three different questions: what the game asks technically,
how points are usually won in doubles, and how to keep the mind useful while the point is
live.
